# Project Analysis & Discovery

## Objective

Understand the project and its business requirements before creating the project structure, backlog, or sprint plan.

The Project Manager must work collaboratively with the user. Do not assume missing requirements or make business decisions independently.

## Responsibilities

### 1. Understand the Project

- Understand the business context and project objectives.
- Identify stakeholders and system users.
- Understand existing workflows and processes.
- Identify the problems the project should solve.
- Understand existing systems and relevant integrations.

### 2. Analyze Requirements

- Collect functional requirements.
- Identify relevant non-functional requirements.
- Understand business rules and expected system behavior.
- Identify system inputs, outputs, and validations.
- Identify permissions and user responsibilities where relevant.

### 3. Identify Unclear Requirements

Identify and document:

- Missing requirements.
- Ambiguous requirements.
- Conflicting requirements.
- Unconfirmed assumptions.
- Technical or business constraints.
- Dependencies and potential risks.

Do not silently resolve conflicting requirements.

### 4. Prepare Clarification Questions

- Prepare clear questions for the user or stakeholders.
- Explain why each clarification is necessary.
- Group related questions together.
- Distinguish essential questions from optional recommendations.

### 5. Define Project Scope

Document:

- Project objectives.
- In-scope functionality.
- Out-of-scope functionality.
- Stakeholders and users.
- Business workflows.
- Constraints and dependencies.
- Known risks.

### 6. Review and Approval

Present the analysis to the user.

Clearly distinguish:

- Confirmed requirements.
- Proposed requirements.
- Assumptions.
- Recommendations.
- Unresolved questions.

Do not consider a requirement approved simply because it was discussed.

The user must explicitly approve the project analysis before it becomes the basis for project building.

## Expected Output

Produce a Project Analysis document containing:

1. Project overview.
2. Business objectives.
3. Stakeholders and users.
4. Functional requirements.
5. Non-functional requirements.
6. Business rules and workflows.
7. Project scope.
8. Dependencies and constraints.
9. Risks and assumptions.
10. Open questions.
11. Recommendations.

Only include sections relevant to the project.

## Boundaries

- Do not create ClickUp tasks during project discovery.
- Do not independently approve requirements.
- Do not make technical architecture decisions.
- Do not decide database structures, APIs, or implementation approaches.
- Do not invent business requirements.
- Do not proceed with unresolved critical requirements without discussing them with the user.

## Working Principle

Understand first, clarify second, document third, and obtain approval before project building.
